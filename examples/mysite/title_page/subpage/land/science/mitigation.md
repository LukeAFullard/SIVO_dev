## Mitigating soil erosion

Based on scientific evidence and after mapping the erodible land within our region we broadly offer three nature-based solutions to mitigate soil erosion: afforestation, retirement and on-farm conservation.   
-	**Afforestation:** Planting native or exotic trees on marginal or non-productive land to provide canopy cover. 
This keeps farms profitable for long term, also provides alternative sources of income by earning carbon credits or setting up a honeybee farm. 
-	Retirement through fencing:  Retiring riparian margins and wetland areas from grazing, which allows growth of native cover including grass to regenerate. A long strip of grass along riparian margins intercepts sediments, preventing sediments to enter the water ways. 
-	On-farm soil conservation:  Planting poplar or willow trees at different intervals on erosion-prone pastureland. This planting method is suitable for lower slopes with sufficient soil and moisture to grow poplar poles. Spaced planting also allows grazing to continue.
  
The focus of all three methods above is to  increase the vegetation cover (See the figure on right on the importance of vegetation cover). These solutions don’t only prevent soil erosion but has additional co benefits.
