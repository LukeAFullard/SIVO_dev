# Land/ Te Whenua
Land is not just our dwelling place but is the foundation of life. Land underpins life on Earth, by supporting various resources including soil and food production, hosts water in its catchments stores ground water, is reserve, ofminerals fossil fuels, sand, and gravel. Land also regulates the climate, filter and regulate water, is a breeding ground for diverse flora and fauna and much more. In short, it supports various ecosystem goods and services important for human wellbeing and survival.

Our region boasts of a diverse landscape ranging from hill country to native forests, to open plains and lowlands, supporting braiding rivers and mountain ranges with coasts on either side of the region. Our landscape flaunts spectacular flora and fauna, and rich fertile soil supporting various economic activities including dairy farming and cropping. It is a delight to live in such a bountiful region.  
However, like other parts of Aotearoa, the land in our region is no stranger to challenges such as soil erosion, wetland loss and declining biodiversity. The natural processes and weather-related events such as rainfall, wind, floods and drought are one of the major contributors to the deteriorating landscape.  However, these natural processes intertwined with human activities such as farming, deforestation, other land-use changes often accelerate and sometimes intensifies the process of degradation. Hence, it is our duty to take care of land as it takes care of us. In Te Ao Maori, there is a strong emphasis on connection between people and its land or whenua and our duty to look after land.
