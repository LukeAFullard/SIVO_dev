# Macroinvertebrates: Tiny Indicators of Stream Health

Macroinvertebrates are small water animals without backbones—such as mayflies, snails, worms, and freshwater crayfish—that live on the bottom of our rivers and streams. While they might be tiny, they play a massive role in the health of our local waterways.

### Why are they important?

**A Crucial Food Source**
These small creatures are a vital part of the underwater food chain. They act as nature's clean-up crew by feeding on dead leaves and algae, and in turn, provide an essential food source for larger animals like fish and wading birds.

**Nature's Warning Sign**
Because different bugs react differently to environmental pressures, they are excellent indicators of overall water quality. Additionally, because they live in the waterway for extended periods, they provide a reliable, long-term record of stream health by integrating the ongoing effects of various environmental factors over time.

### What hurts our water bugs?

**Low-Level Nutrient Threats**
Nutrients like nitrogen do not need to reach directly toxic levels to harm our water bugs. Even at lower levels, excess nutrients act as a super-food that fuels massive weed and algal blooms, smothering where these invertebrates live. When this overgrown plant life eventually dies and rots, the decomposition process sucks vital oxygen out of the water, essentially suffocating sensitive aquatic insects.

**Loss of Sensitive Species**
Certain insects, like mayflies and stoneflies, are highly sensitive to pollution and can only thrive in clean, healthy water. When rivers become polluted by excess dirt, algae, or nutrient enrichment, these sensitive bugs are often the first to disappear.

**A Shift in the Ecosystem**
When water quality drops, only tough, pollution-tolerant bugs like worms and snails can survive. A healthy stream needs a wide variety of insect life. If scientists only find worms and snails, it is a clear warning sign that the waterway is degraded and struggling.
