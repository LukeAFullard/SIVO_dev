# Phosphorus

Phosphorus is a naturally occurring nutrient that helps aquatic plants grow. However, just like nitrogen, excessive phosphorus in our rivers and lakes disrupts the natural balance of freshwater ecosystems. 

### Why is it a problem?

- **Out-of-control plant and algae growth**
    * High levels of phosphorus act as a powerful fertiliser in the water. The nutrient fuels the rapid, explosive growth of aquatic weeds and harmful algal blooms. 

### How does it get into our water?
Phosphorus enters our waterways through both natural processes and human activities: 

-   **Geology:**
    * Phosphorus occurs naturally in rocks, minerals, and soils. In some areas, the local geology naturally releases higher levels of phosphorus into the water. 
-   **Soil erosion:**
    * Phosphorus binds very tightly to soil particles. When soil washes into rivers during heavy rain, it carries this trapped phosphorus with it. 
-   **Overland runoff:**
    * Rainwater flowing across the land can pick up excess agricultural fertiliser and poorly timed effluent applications, washing them directly into rivers and streams. 
-   **Wastewater discharges:**
    * Phosphorus is a common component of human and animal waste, and it can enter our waterways directly through domestic wastewater and stormwater discharges.
