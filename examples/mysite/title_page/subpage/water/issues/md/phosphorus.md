# Phosphorus: Fuel for Weeds and Algae

Phosphorus is an essential natural nutrient that helps aquatic plants grow. However, just like nitrogen, having too much phosphorus in our rivers and lakes disrupts the natural balance of the ecosystem.

### Why is it a problem?

**Out of Control Growth**
High levels of phosphorus act as a powerful fertiliser in the water. This excess nutrient fuels the rapid, explosive growth of aquatic weeds and harmful algal blooms.

**Choking Aquatic Life**
When massive algal blooms take over a waterway, they can literally choke fish and sensitive water bugs. This overgrown plant life causes long-term damage to the overall health of our surface water.

### How does it get into our water?
Phosphorus enters our waterways through both natural processes and human activities:

*   **Natural Geology:** Phosphorus occurs naturally in rocks, minerals, and soils. In some areas, the local geology and groundwater naturally release higher levels of phosphorus into the water.
*   **Soil Erosion:** Phosphorus binds very tightly to dirt particles. When soil washes into rivers during heavy rain, it carries this trapped phosphorus with it. 
*   **Overland Runoff:** Rainwater flowing across the land can pick up excess agricultural fertiliser and poorly timed effluent applications, washing them directly into rivers and streams.
*   **Wastewater Discharges:** Phosphorus is a common component of human and animal waste, and it can enter our waterways directly through domestic wastewater and stormwater discharges.
