# Algae

Algae are naturally occurring organisms that live in our rivers and lakes. While algae is a normal part of the food chain, an overgrowth of algae can cause major problems for our waterways. 

To understand how much algae is in the water, scientists measure chlorophyll-a, which is the green pigment that plants and algae use for photosynthesis.  

### Why is it a problem?

- **Smothers habitats**
    * Excessive algae growth can form thick, slimy mats on riverbeds or turn lake water cloudy and green. These overgrowths can physically smother the natural habitat where fish and aquatic insects live. 
- **Impacts recreational activities**
    * Thick overgrowths of algae can negatively affect the natural beauty of our waterways and make them unpleasant for recreation. Slimy algal mats can make the riverbed slippery for swimmers and waders. Long, hair-like strands of filamentous algae can be a nuisance for anglers, as the slimy strands get caught in their lines, disrupting an enjoyable day on the water. 
- **Risk to humans and pets**
    * Some potentially toxic forms of algae, known as cyanobacteria, can be harmful to human and animal health (especially dogs), making the water unsuitable for play. 

### Where does it come from?
While algae are natural, rapid overgrowth is fueled by human activities. Blooms thrive when there are excess nutrients (like nitrogen and phosphorus) in the water, combined with warm temperatures, high sunlight, and low river flows that fail to flush the algae away.
