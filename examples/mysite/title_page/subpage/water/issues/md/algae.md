# Algae and Toxic Algae: A Slimy Threat

Algae are naturally occurring microscopic organisms that live in our rivers and lakes. While a small amount is a normal part of the food chain, an overgrowth of algae can cause major problems for our waterways.

To understand exactly how much algae is in the water, scientists measure **chlorophyll-a**, which is the green pigment that plants and algae use to grow. High levels of chlorophyll-a act as a warning sign that a large algal bloom is happening.

### Why is it a problem?

**Smothering Habitats**
Fast-growing algae can form thick, slimy mats on riverbeds or turn lake water cloudy and green. This massive overgrowth physically smothers the natural homes where fish and vital water bugs live.

**Toxic Threats**
Some specific types of algae, known scientifically as cyanobacteria, can produce dangerous natural toxins. In rivers, toxic algae typically form dark brown or black mats on rocks. These mats can break off and wash up on the river edge, where they dry out and produce a strong, musty smell. 

**Danger to Dogs and Humans**
Touching or swallowing water that contains toxic algae can make people seriously ill. It is especially dangerous for dogs, who are naturally attracted to the musty smell of the dried mats. Eating even a tiny piece of toxic algae the size of a 50-cent coin can be fatal for a dog.
