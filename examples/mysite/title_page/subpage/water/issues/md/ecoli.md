# *E. coli*

*E. coli* is a type of bacteria naturally found in the guts of warm-blooded animals, including humans. While these bacteria are often not harmful in themselves, they act as a crucial warning sign for the health of our local waterways.

### Why is it a problem?
High levels of *E. coli* indicate that a waterway has been contaminated by faecal matter (poo). If *E. coli* is present, it is highly likely that other harmful viruses, protozoa, and disease-causing bacteria are also lurking in the water. Swimming or playing in contaminated water can make you sick, significantly increasing the risk of gastroenteritis and skin, ear, eye, and respiratory infections. 

### How does it get into our water?
These bacteria wash into our rivers, lakes, and coastal waters through everyday environmental pressures and weather events. The most common sources include: 
*   Animal and bird droppings.
*   Stock access in waterbodies.
*   Heavy rain washing over the land, known as overland flow.
*   Discharges from local wastewater and stormwater systems.
*   Poorly managed on-site wastewater systems.

For further information you may like to check out the [LAWA Factsheet](https://www.lawa.org.nz/learn/factsheets/faecal-indicators) on faecal indicator bacteria.
