# The Rangitīkei-Turakina FMU

The Rangitīkei-Turakina Freshwater Management Unit (FMU) is home to the mighty Rangitīkei River and the Turakina River, along with their many tributaries, lakes, and wetlands. As the fifth-longest river in New Zealand, the Rangitīkei travels from its mountainous headwaters all the way to the Tasman Sea. It is a highly popular adventure destination where people love to raft, jet boat, swim, and fish beneath iconic papa cliffs. Meanwhile, the Turakina River meanders through rural hill country before reaching the coast near Koitiata.

According to Māori tradition, the explorer Haunui-a-Nanaia named these rivers during his journey down the west coast. *Rangitīkei* translates to "the day of striding out," because he walked for a full day (*rangi*) with long strides (*tīkei*) to reach its waters. He named the *Turakina* River because he was able to cross it using a tree that had fallen from one bank to the other.
