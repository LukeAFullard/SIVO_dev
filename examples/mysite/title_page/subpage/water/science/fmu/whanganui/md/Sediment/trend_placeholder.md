Trends show us if water clarity is consistently changing over time. We use a 10-year timeframe to see past temporary weather spikes—like heavy rain that naturally washes more soil into the river—and uncover the true, long-term direction of water quality.

<br><br>
<strong>Trend Categories</strong>
<br>
Trends are grouped by how confident scientists are that a change is actually happening. For suspended sediment and visual clarity, an "improvement" means the water is getting clearer and visibility is increasing.

<ul>
<li><strong>Improving:</strong> Water clarity is consistently increasing (<em>very likely</em> or <em>likely improving</em>).</li>
<li><strong>Degrading:</strong> Water clarity is consistently decreasing, posing a higher risk to aquatic life (<em>very likely</em> or <em>likely degrading</em>).</li>
<li><strong>Indeterminate:</strong> There is not enough statistical evidence to confirm a direction.</li>
</ul>
