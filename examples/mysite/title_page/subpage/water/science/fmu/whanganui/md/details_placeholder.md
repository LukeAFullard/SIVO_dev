# The Whanganui FMU

As the second-longest river in the North Island, the Whanganui River forms the largest river system in our region. It begins its journey on the north-western slopes of Mount Tongariro, flowing north before turning at Taumarunui. From there, it weaves its way south through spectacular native forests and the Tongariro and Whanganui National Parks, before finally reaching the Tasman Sea at the city of Whanganui. 

The river channel is famous for its dramatic, high-sided cliffs made of sand and mudstone, which sit within an erosion-prone hill country landscape. For local iwi, the river is deeply revered and the stories of the river and the humans who live alongside it are intertwined. This profound connection is captured in the well-known saying: *"E rere kau mai te Āwanui, Mai i te Kāhui maunga ki Tangaroa. Kō au te Āwa, kō te Āwa kō au"*, The great river flows from the mountains to the sea. I am the river, the river is me.
