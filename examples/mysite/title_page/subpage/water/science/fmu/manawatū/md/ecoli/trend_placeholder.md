### Understanding the Trend
While the "state" gives us a current snapshot of water health, calculating a trend helps us understand the bigger picture. It reveals whether the amount of *E. coli* in the water is consistently changing over time. 

Because bacteria levels naturally fluctuate with the seasons and heavy rainfall, scientists analyse data collected over a 10-year timeframe. This long-term view helps us see past temporary weather events to uncover the true underlying direction of the water quality.

### How We Categorise Change
Trend results are grouped into categories based on how confident scientists are that a change is actually happening. For *E. coli*, it is important to remember that an "improvement" means the bacteria levels are going down.

*   **Improving:** This means *E. coli* levels are consistently decreasing, which is a positive sign for our water quality. This is reported as either *very likely improving* or *likely improving*.
*   **Degrading:** This means *E. coli* levels are consistently increasing, posing a higher risk to human health. This is reported as either *very likely degrading* or *likely degrading*.
*   **Indeterminate:** There is not enough strong statistical evidence to say for sure if the bacteria levels are going up or down.
*   **Not Assessed:** The site did not have enough consistent data over the 10-year period to calculate a reliable trend.
