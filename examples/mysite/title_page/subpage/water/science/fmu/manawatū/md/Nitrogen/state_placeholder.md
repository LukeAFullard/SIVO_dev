### How We Measure and Grade
Scientists measure the concentration of nitrogen in the water in milligrams per litre (mg/L). 

To calculate a reliable A-to-D ecological health grade for toxicity, experts evaluate two different statistics calculated from data collected over a five-year period:
*   **Median:** The middle value, representing the typical amount of nitrogen present on an average day.
*   **95th percentile:** A measure of the extremes, representing the highest nitrogen levels expected on a "bad" day.

A site must pass the test for both of these statistics. The final grade is always determined by the worst-performing statistic. 

*   **Band A:** Represents high conservation value, where sensitive species are unlikely to be harmed. 
*   **Band D:** Represents significant pollution, where nitrogen levels are high enough to impact growth or approach acute toxicity (risk of death) for sensitive fish and aquatic insects.
