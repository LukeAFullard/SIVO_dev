# The Manawatū Freshwater Management Unit

Welcome to the Manawatū Freshwater Management Unit (FMU). This is the specific geographic area we use to monitor and manage the Manawatū River, its tributaries, and local lakes and wetlands.

### A Unique Journey
The Manawatū River is truly unique—it is the only river in New Zealand that completely crosses a mountain range. It begins on the eastern side of the Ruahine Ranges, cuts through the stunning Te Āpiti (Manawatū Gorge), and flows across the plains before finally meeting the Tasman Sea at Foxton Beach. 

According to Māori tradition, the river's name tells a story. When the explorer Haunui-a-Nanaia reached its wide, cold waters, it made him catch his breath. In te reo Māori, *manawa* means breath, and *tū* means to stand still. 

### Pressures on Our Water
The Manawatū area is a diverse patchwork of pastoral farms, native forests, and busy urban centres like Palmerston North and Feilding. Because our lives are so intertwined with the river, our everyday activities place significant pressure on the health of the water. 

The main challenges facing the Manawatū FMU today include:
*   **Pollution:** Wastewater and stormwater discharges carry harmful bacteria and excess nutrients directly into our waterways.
*   **Water Abstraction:** Taking freshwater from rivers and groundwater for human use, including large hydroelectric power schemes, reduces the water available for aquatic life to survive.
*   **Land Use:** Everyday activities like farming and urban development can cause soil, bacteria, and nutrients to wash off the land and into the river system.

As the local proverb reminds us: *"If the water is healthy, the land and the people are nourished"*.
