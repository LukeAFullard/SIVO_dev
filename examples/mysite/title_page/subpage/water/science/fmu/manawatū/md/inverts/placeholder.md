# Tracking Macroinvertebrates in the Manawatū

Macroinvertebrates are small animals without backbones—such as insect larvae, snails, worms, and freshwater crayfish (kōura)—that live on or just below the riverbed. These tiny creatures play a central role in stream ecosystems by eating algae and dead leaves, and they serve as a vital food source for fish and native birds. 

### Why They Matter
Because some insects (like mayflies and stoneflies) need pristine water to survive, while others (like worms and snails) can tolerate heavy pollution, the mix of species found in a river acts as an excellent natural warning system for water health. Their populations decline when waterways suffer from organic pollution, nutrient enrichment, or lost habitats.
