Trends show us if the ecological health of the riverbed is consistently changing over time. We use a 10-year timeframe to see past temporary events—such as floods that can temporarily wash these insects away—and uncover the true, long-term direction of the ecosystem's health.

<br><br>
<strong>Trend Categories</strong>
<br>
Trends are grouped by how confident scientists are that a change is actually happening. For the MCI, an "improvement" means the index score is increasing and sensitive insect populations are recovering.

<ul>
<li><strong>Improving:</strong> The MCI score is consistently increasing, showing a recovering ecosystem (<em>very likely</em> or <em>likely improving</em>).</li>
<li><strong>Degrading:</strong> The MCI score is consistently decreasing, meaning the river is becoming more polluted and sensitive species are dying off (<em>very likely</em> or <em>likely degrading</em>).</li>
<li><strong>Indeterminate:</strong> There is not enough statistical evidence to confirm a direction.</li>
</ul>
