# Nitrogen: Too Much of a Good Thing

Nitrogen is a naturally occurring nutrient that is absolutely essential for plants to grow. However, when too much nitrogen enters our rivers, lakes, and streams, it throws the entire ecosystem out of balance.

### Why is it a problem?

**Fuels Plant and Algae Growth**
Excess nitrogen acts like a super-food in our waterways, fueling the rapid and problematic growth of aquatic weeds and algae.

**Toxic to Aquatic Life**
At extremely high levels, specific types of nitrogen (like ammonia and nitrate) become directly toxic to fish and sensitive water bugs.

**Harmful to Drinking Water**
High nitrate levels can also pose a serious health risk to humans and livestock if the nitrogen contaminates our groundwater and drinking water supplies.

### How does it get into our water?
Nitrogen is highly soluble, meaning it easily dissolves in water and travels quickly through the environment. The most common sources include:
*   Excess fertiliser washing off agricultural paddocks.
*   Animal waste, particularly cow urine, soaking through the soil and into groundwater.
*   Direct discharges from wastewater treatment plants and leaky septic systems.
